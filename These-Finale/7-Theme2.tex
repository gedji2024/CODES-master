\section{Abstract}
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) deployed in disaster-prone environments must extend network lifetime and maintain reliable data delivery while reducing their full-lifecycle carbon footprint.
To date, no protocol jointly optimizes Lifecycle Carbon Intensity (LCI)---defined as the embodied, operational, and end-of-life carbon dioxide associated with each useful packet.
We propose CALASH (Carbon-Aware Lifecycle-Autonomous Self-Healing), a framework integrating four pillars: (i)~Carbon-Aware Data Reduction (CADR), which modulates compressive-sensing ratios with grid carbon intensity; (ii)~Carbon-Aware Routing Engine (CARE), a hybrid Lyapunov--DQN router with provable carbon-budget guarantees; (iii)~Self-Healing Disaster Recovery (SHDR) via an autonomic MAPE-K loop; and (iv)~Lifecycle Sustainability Engine (LSE) embedding ISO~14040-compliant end-of-life costs into routing.
CALASH operates over a sub-THz/sub-6~GHz dual-band 6G physical layer.
Extensive simulations validated against real Intel~Lab traces and UK grid carbon data show that CALASH achieves 60\% longer lifetime (1,486 vs.\ 931 rounds), 82.9\% Packet Delivery Ratio, and 33\% lower LCI (8.99 vs.\ 13.42 gCO2eq/pkt) compared to LEACH, outperforming all six traditional baselines with statistical significance.
An ablation study reveals CADR as the dominant pillar (59\% LCI reduction), while 6G technologies contribute a 10.5\% lifetime gain.


\section{Introduction}
\label{art2:sec:introduction}

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are collections of battery-powered motes that sense physical phenomena and relay data to a Base Station (BS) via multi-hop communication.
With non-rechargeable energy budgets (e.g.\ 0.5\,J per Mica2 mote~\cite{heinzelman2002leach}), maximizing network lifetime while ensuring reliable data delivery is a central challenge.

\textbf{Why carbon matters.} The ICT sector contributes 2--4\% of global GHG emissions, and the IoT is among its fastest-growing segments~\cite{freitag2021ict, huynh2025carbon_dt}. When thousands of sensor nodes are manufactured, powered, and discarded, their combined carbon footprint becomes significant. Most of this footprint comes from \emph{embodied carbon}: the CO$_2$ emitted during chip fabrication and logistics~\cite{pirson2021embodied}. A typical IoT node embodies ${\sim}10$\,kg\,CO$_2$eq~\cite{pirson2021embodied}, while its operational energy generates only ${\sim}0.01$\,g\,CO$_2$eq. Thus, \textbf{maximizing packets delivered before network death is the most effective way to reduce per-packet carbon footprint}. We formalize this insight via \emph{Lifecycle Carbon Intensity} (LCI):
\begin{equation}
    \text{LCI} = \frac{C_{\text{embodied}} + C_{\text{operational}} + C_{\text{end-of-life}}}{\text{total useful packets delivered}} \quad [\text{gCO}_2\text{eq/pkt}]
    \label{art2:eq:lci}
\end{equation}
where $C_{embodied}$, $C_{operational}$, $C_{end-of-life}$ respectively represent the manufacturing carbon of all sensors, the carbon from electricity used during routing, and the carbon from disposing dead sensors (e-waste).

\textbf{Why disaster resilience matters.}
In disaster-prone deployments~\cite{erdelj2017disaster, karaman2025disaster_survey}, a single seismic event can disable 30--50\% of nodes, breaking routing and wasting their embodied carbon.

Table~\ref{art2:tab:gap_summary} summarizes the capabilities of representative baselines.
\emph{No existing protocol simultaneously addresses} (i)~carbon-aware adaptive data reduction, (ii)~provable carbon-budget routing with online learning, (iii)~autonomic disaster self-healing, and (iv)~lifecycle carbon integration in the routing objective.

\section{Related Work}
\label{art2:sec:related}

We organize the literature into four areas.

\subsection{Energy-Efficient Clustering Protocols}
LEACH~\cite{heinzelman2000leach} introduced probabilistic CH election via a rotating threshold.
EE-LEACH~\cite{bakaraniya2013eeleach} refines this by incorporating residual energy, while HEED~\cite{younis2004heed} uses distributed iterative selection based on energy and communication cost.
Singh et al.~\cite{singh2017survey} review over 30 LEACH variants; Kaur et al.~\cite{kaur2025energy}~(2025) confirm that energy efficiency remains the dominant optimization goal.
\textbf{Limitation}: none of these protocols account for grid carbon intensity, compressive sensing, or disaster recovery.

\subsection{Metaheuristic and RL-Based Routing}
ABC-ACO~\cite{elkhediri2024abcaco} combines ABC~\cite{karaboga2007abc} and ACO~\cite{dorigo2004ant} for joint CH selection and multi-hop routing.
EERP~\cite{biswas2018eerp} uses residual energy and distance for relay selection.
Q-Routing~\cite{boyan1994qrouting} applies tabular Q-learning~\cite{watkins1992qlearning} for online next-hop selection.
More recent work includes ML-based clustering~\cite{akram2025eemlcr} and fuzzy-DRL routing~\cite{wang2025fuzzy_drl}.
\textbf{Limitation}: ABC-ACO is limited to 3~iterations per round online~\cite{kuila2014abc_wsn}; none incorporate carbon awareness or provide convergence guarantees under carbon-budget constraints.

\subsection{Carbon-Aware Networking}
Carbon-aware computing---scheduling workloads during low-CI grid periods---is well established in cloud research~\cite{urgaonkar2011green, freitag2021ict}, with recent extensions to edge computing~\cite{huynh2025carbon_dt} and 6G task offloading~\cite{navaprakash2026carbon}.
However, its application to resource-constrained WSNs remains unexplored.
The UK grid CI varies from ${\sim}50$ to ${\sim}400$\,gCO$_2$/kWh~\cite{uk_carbon_api, ember2024}.
To the best of our knowledge, \emph{no prior work adapts routing and compression decisions using real-time carbon intensity}.

\subsection{Disaster-Resilient WSNs and Lifecycle Assessment}
Erdelj et al.~\cite{erdelj2017disaster} survey disaster-management WSN deployments; Alsayyed et al.~\cite{alsayyed2025disaster} and Karaman et al.~\cite{karaman2025disaster_survey} extend this to 6G-era networks.
Self-healing via backup CH election exists but not jointly with carbon-aware routing.
LifeCycle Assessment (LCA), per ISO~14040~\cite{iso14040}/14044~\cite{iso14044}, quantifies environmental impacts across a product's full life cycle.
Pirson and Bol~\cite{pirson2021embodied} showed that IoT embodied carbon exceeds operational carbon by 3--5 orders of magnitude.
\textbf{No prior WSN routing protocol incorporates LCA into its routing objective.}

\section{Methodology}
\label{art2:sec:methodology}

We consider $N$ sensor nodes deployed uniformly at random in a $L \times L$\,m$^2$ area.
A single Base Station (BS) is located at coordinates $(x, y)$, outside the top edge of the sensing field---a standard WSN benchmark configuration~\cite{heinzelman2002leach}.
Each node has an initial energy budget of $E_0$\,J.
A node is considered \emph{dead} when its energy reaches zero; it can no longer sense, transmit, or receive.

\subsection{System Model}
\label{art2:sec:system_model}

\textbf{First-order radio energy model.}
All protocols in our framework use the same energy model for fair comparison~\cite{heinzelman2000leach, rappaport2002wireless}:
\begin{align}
    E_{\text{Tx}}(l, d) &= l \cdot E_{\text{elec}} + l \cdot
    \begin{cases}
        \varepsilon_{\text{fs}} \cdot d^2 & \text{if } d < d_0 \\
        \varepsilon_{\text{mp}} \cdot d^4 & \text{if } d \geq d_0
    \end{cases}
    \label{art2:eq:etx} \\
    E_{\text{Rx}}(l) &= l \cdot E_{\text{elec}}
    \label{art2:eq:erx}
\end{align}
where $l$ is the number of bits, $d$ is the distance in meters, $E_{\text{elec}} = 50$\,nJ/bit is the electronics energy, $\varepsilon_{\text{fs}} = 10$\,pJ/bit/m$^2$ is the free-space amplifier coefficient, $\varepsilon_{\text{mp}} = 0.0013$\,pJ/bit/m$^4$ is the multipath amplifier coefficient, and $d_0 = \sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{fs}}/\varepsilon_{\text{mp}}} \approx 87.7$\,m is the crossover distance.

\textbf{Carbon intensity model.}
The operational carbon emitted when a node consumes $E$\,Joules of energy at round $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
    C_{\text{op}}(E, t) = E \cdot \frac{1}{3.6 \times 10^6} \cdot \text{CI}(t)
    \label{art2:eq:carbon_op}
\end{equation}
where $\text{CI}(t)$ is the grid carbon intensity in gCO$_2$eq/kWh at round $t$ and $1/(3.6 \times 10^6)$ converts Joules to kWh.
Three CI sources are supported: (1)~real UK traces (8,760~h, mean~152\,gCO$_2$/kWh)~\cite{uk_carbon_api}; (2)~calibrated Ember~2024 profiles for five countries~\cite{ember2024}; (3)~default synthetic (base~200, amplitude~80, $\sigma{=}25$, clipped to $[20,600]$).

\textbf{Disaster model} 
We model disasters as Gaussian spatial failure events.
At round $t_d$, an event centered at $(x_c, y_c)$ with damage radius $r_d$ destroys every node within $r_d$ meters, and applies graduated damage to nodes further away based on distance.
Damage radii are calibrated to USGS ShakeMap MMI$\geq$VIII data~\cite{usgs_turkey2023, worden2005shakemap}: localized ($r_d{=}50$\,m, $\sim$15\%), moderate ($r_d{=}100$\,m, $\sim$30\%, default), and severe ($r_d{=}150$\,m, $\sim$50\%).

\textbf{Lifecycle carbon accounting.}
Following ISO~14040~\cite{iso14040} and Pirson \& Bol~\cite{pirson2021embodied}, each node carries: embodied carbon $C_{\text{emb}} = 10{,}000$\,gCO$_2$eq (fabrication through transport); operational carbon $C_{\text{op}}$ per Eq.~\eqref{art2:eq:carbon_op}; and end-of-life carbon $C_{\text{eol}} = 500(1 - r_{\text{recycle}})$\,gCO$_2$eq per dead node ($r_{\text{recycle}}{=}0.3$)~\cite{forti2024ewaste}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{art2:sec:problem}

\textbf{Objective.}
Minimize the Lifecycle Carbon Intensity over a mission of $T$ rounds:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\boldsymbol{\pi}} \quad \text{LCI}(\boldsymbol{\pi}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} C_{\text{emb}}^{(i)} + \sum_{t=1}^{T} C_{\text{op}}(t) + \sum_{i:\text{dead}} C_{\text{eol}}^{(i)}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T} D(t)}
    \label{art2:eq:lci_formal}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ is the joint routing and compression policy, and $D(t)$ is the number of useful packets delivered to the BS at round $t$.

\textbf{Constraints:}
\begin{align}
    & E_i(t) \geq 0 \quad \forall i, t  &\text{(energy non-negativity)} \\
    & \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} C_{\text{op}}(t) \leq \bar{c} + B/V  &\text{(Lyapunov carbon bound)} \\
    & \text{NMSE}(\rho) \leq \delta \quad &\text{(data fidelity threshold)}
\end{align}
where $\bar{c}+B/V$ is the Lyapunov carbon budget ($V$ trades delivery emphasis against strict compliance), and $\delta$ is the acceptable distortion threshold for compressed data.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Network $\mathcal{G}$, round $t$, config $\theta$
\Ensure Delivered packets, energy consumed, carbon emitted
\Comment{Phase 0: Self-Healing Monitor}
\If{$t > t_d$ \textbf{and} recovery\_mode}
    \State Run MAPE-K heartbeat check
    \State Check if recovery complete ($> 50$ rounds since disaster)
\EndIf
\Comment{Phase 1: Carbon-Modulated CH Election (Setup)}
\State $\text{CI}(t) \gets \text{CarbonTraceManager.get\_ci}(t)$
\State $\text{CI}_{\text{norm}} \gets (\text{CI}(t) - \text{CI}_{\min}) / (\text{CI}_{\max} - \text{CI}_{\min})$
\State $f_c \gets \max(1 - \beta \cdot \text{CI}_{\text{norm}}, \; 0.1)$
\ForAll{alive node $s_i$ eligible this epoch}
    \State $T_{\text{base}} \gets p / (1 - p \cdot (r \bmod 1/p))$ \Comment{LEACH threshold}
    \State $T_i \gets T_{\text{base}} \cdot (E_i / E_0) \cdot f_c$
    \If{$\text{rand}() < T_i$} \State Elect $s_i$ as CH \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Enforce $\geq 5\%$ alive nodes as CHs
\State Form clusters (nearest-CH assignment)
\Comment{Phase 2: CADR -- Adaptive Compression \& Intra-Cluster TX}
\State $\rho(t) \gets$ reference-point CADR mapping (Eq.~\ref{art2:eq:rho})
\If{recovery\_mode} \State $\rho(t) \gets \rho_{\max}$ \EndIf
\State $m \gets \lceil \rho(t) \cdot n \rceil$ \Comment{compressed signal dimension}
\ForAll{cluster member $s_j$}
    \State Sense signal, compress to $m$ measurements
    \State Transmit $m \cdot l_{\text{bit}}$ bits to CH (THz if $d < 30$\,m)
\EndFor
\Comment{Phase 3: CARE -- Inter-Cluster Routing to BS}
\ForAll{cluster head $\text{CH}_k$}
    \State Aggregate received data
    \If{DQN trained and not recovery\_mode}
        \State route $\gets$ DQN.select\_multi\_hop($\text{CH}_k$, BS)
    \Else
        \State route $\gets$ Lyapunov.compute\_route($\text{CH}_k$, BS)
    \EndIf
    \State Transmit along route (sub-6\,GHz inter-cluster)
    \State Store DQN experience, train if buffer $\geq 256$
\EndFor
\Comment{Phase 4: Carbon Accounting \& Queue Update}
\State $C_{\text{op}}(t) \gets E_{\text{total}} \cdot J_{\text{kWh}} \cdot \text{CI}(t)$
\State $Z(t+1) \gets \max\{0, Z(t) + C_{\text{op}}(t) - \bar{c}\}$
\State \Return packets delivered, $E_{\text{total}}$, $C_{\text{op}}(t)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Pillar 1: CADR --- Carbon-Aware Data Reduction}
\label{art2:sec:cadr}

\textbf{Core idea.}
Instead of transmitting all sensed data, each cluster member compresses its signal using Compressive Sensing (CS)~\cite{candes2006cs, baraniuk2007cs}.
The compression ratio $\rho(t) \in [\rho_{\min}, \rho_{\max}]$ (the fraction of the original signal that is actually transmitted) is adjusted dynamically based on the current carbon intensity using a \emph{reference-point mapping} centred at $\text{CI}_{\text{base}}$ (the grid's average CI):

\begin{equation}
    \rho(t) = \begin{cases}
        \rho_{\max} - (\rho_{\max} - \rho_{\text{mid}}) \cdot \frac{\text{CI}(t) - \text{CI}_{\min}}{\text{CI}_{\text{base}} - \text{CI}_{\min}} & \text{if } \text{CI}(t) \leq \text{CI}_{\text{base}} \\[6pt]
        \rho_{\text{mid}} - (\rho_{\text{mid}} - \rho_{\min}) \cdot \frac{\text{CI}(t) - \text{CI}_{\text{base}}}{\text{CI}_{\max} - \text{CI}_{\text{base}}} & \text{if } \text{CI}(t) > \text{CI}_{\text{base}}
    \end{cases}
    \label{art2:eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\text{mid}} = (\rho_{\min} + \rho_{\max}) / 2$.
This piecewise mapping centres $\rho = 0.55$ at the grid-average CI, avoiding the asymmetry of a simple linear mapping.

During disaster recovery, $\rho$ is overridden to $\rho_{\max}$ to preserve critical data.

\textbf{Compressive sensing pipeline.}
An $s$-sparse signal $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is recovered from $m{=}\rho n$ Gaussian measurements via OMP~\cite{candes2006cs, tropp2007omp}; fidelity $F = 1 - \text{NMSE}$.
The Intel Lab traces~\cite{intel_lab_data} are ${\sim}1$-sparse in the DCT domain, achieving $F > 0.99$ at $\rho = 0.3$.

\subsubsection{Pillar 2: CARE --- Carbon-Aware Routing Engine}
\label{art2:sec:care}

CARE combines two complementary routing strategies:

\textbf{(a) Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty routing (provable guarantees).}
We maintain a \emph{virtual carbon queue} $Z(t)$ that tracks cumulative carbon overshoot~\cite{neely2010lyapunov}:
\begin{equation}
    Z(t+1) = \max\big\{0, \; Z(t) + C_{\text{op}}(t) - \bar{c}\big\}
    \label{art2:eq:queue_update}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c} = C_{\text{budget}} / T$ is the per-round carbon budget.
At each hop, the next relay $j^*$ is chosen to minimize:
\begin{equation}
    j^* = \arg\min_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \Big[\underbrace{Z(t) \cdot c_{ij}(t)}_{\text{carbon pressure}} + \underbrace{V \cdot e_{ij}}_{\text{energy cost}}\Big] \cdot \underbrace{\ell_j}_{\text{lifecycle penalty}}
    \label{art2:eq:lyapunov_decision}
\end{equation}
where $c_{ij}(t)$ is the hop carbon cost, $e_{ij}$ is the energy cost, $V$ is the Lyapunov tradeoff parameter, and $\ell_j = 1 + w_{\text{eol}}(1 - E_j/E_0)^2$ penalizes low-energy nodes to avoid wasting their embodied carbon.

\textbf{(b) Deep Q-Network (DQN) routing (autonomous adaptation).}
After 256 replay-buffer transitions, a NumPy-only Double DQN~\cite{mnih2015dqn, vanhasselt2016ddqn} ($9 \to 64 \to 32 \to 1$, ReLU) takes over routing.
It scores each candidate next-hop and selects the highest Q-value; the 9D state encodes source/candidate energy fractions, distances, normalized CI, carbon queue pressure, alive ratio, and lifecycle factor.

The reward function captures the multi-objective nature of CALASH:
\begin{equation}
    r = \underbrace{1.0 \cdot \frac{\Delta d_{\text{BS}}}{d_{\max}}}_{\text{delivery progress}}
      - \underbrace{0.3 \cdot \text{CI}_{\text{norm}} \cdot E_{\text{Tx}} \cdot J_{\text{kWh}}}_{\text{carbon penalty}}
      - \underbrace{0.3 \cdot \frac{E_{\text{Tx}}}{E_{\text{src}}}}_{\text{energy penalty}}
      - \underbrace{0.2 \cdot \max(0, \ell - 1)}_{\text{lifecycle penalty}}
      + \underbrace{5.0 \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{BS}}}_{\text{delivery bonus}}
      - \underbrace{2.0 \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{drop}}}_{\text{drop penalty}}
    \label{art2:eq:reward}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Fallback strategy.}
During disaster recovery, the DQN is bypassed (its policy may be outdated for the new topology), and the Lyapunov router takes over until the network stabilizes.

\subsubsection{Pillar 3: SHDR --- Self-Healing Disaster Recovery}
\label{art2:sec:shdr}

SHDR implements the Monitor-Analyze-Plan-Execute with Knowledge (MAPE-K) autonomic computing loop~\cite{kephart2003autonomic}:
(1)~\textbf{Monitor}: alive nodes send periodic heartbeat messages (50~bits, every 2~rounds; every round during recovery). Missing 3 consecutive heartbeats marks a node as dead.
(2)~\textbf{Analyze}: identify coverage holes and orphan nodes.
(3)~\textbf{Plan}: emergency CH election promotes highest-energy survivors; damaged nodes ($E < 0.3 E_0$) have election probability reduced by 90\%.
(4)~\textbf{Execute}: new topology takes effect for 50~rounds.
During healing, CH density increases by 50\% and CADR switches to $\rho_{\max}$.

\subsubsection{Pillar 4: LSE --- Lifecycle Sustainability Engine}
\label{art2:sec:lse}

LSE embeds lifecycle carbon costs into the routing objective:
\begin{equation}
    \ell_j = 1 + w_{\text{eol}} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{E_j}{E_0}\right)^2
    \label{art2:eq:lifecycle_penalty}
\end{equation}
where $w_{\text{eol}} = 1.0$ is the end-of-life penalty weight.
Nodes near death ($E_j \approx 0$) get $\ell_j \approx 2$, discouraging routing through nodes whose embodied carbon would be wasted.

\subsection{6G Enabling Technologies}
\label{art2:sec:6g_enabling}

\subsubsection{Sub-THz Communication with OFDM}
\label{art2:sec:thz}

Intra-cluster links use $f{=}140$\,GHz, $B{=}10$\,GHz~\cite{rappaport2019thz} with an OFDM waveform (480\,kHz SCS, 2{,}048 sub-carriers, 7\% CP), yielding throughput efficiency $\eta_{\text{OFDM}}{=}0.93$ and 3\,dB PAPR back-off.
The effective channel capacity is
\begin{equation}
    C_{\text{eff}} = \eta_{\text{OFDM}} \cdot B \log_2\!\left(1 + \frac{P_{\text{Tx}} \cdot |h|^2}{N_0 B}\right)
    \label{art2:eq:thz_capacity}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the channel coefficient, and the transmit power includes PAPR headroom $P_{\text{Tx}}^{\text{PA}} = P_{\text{Tx}} \cdot 10^{3/10}$.

\subsubsection{Cascaded RIS Channel Model}
\label{art2:sec:ris}

A Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS) with $N_{\text{RIS}} = 64$ elements assists the sub-THz link when direct line-of-sight is obstructed (e.g., by disaster debris).
The cascaded Tx$\to$RIS$\to$Rx channel gain is modelled as~\cite{huang2021ris, huang2020ris_drl, alhilo2024ris_uav}:
\begin{equation}
    G_{\text{RIS}} = 20\log_{10}(N_{\text{RIS}}) + G_{\text{CSI}} + G_{\text{beam}} - L_{\text{coupling}}
    \label{art2:eq:ris_gain}
\end{equation}
where $G_{\text{CSI}}{=}10\log_{10}(0.90^2)$ captures estimation error, $G_{\text{beam}}{=}{-}0.01$\,dB models beam misalignment~\cite{alhilo2024ris_uav}, and $L_{\text{coupling}}{=}1$\,dB accounts for mutual coupling~\cite{yang2021ris_jamming}, reducing the effective gain from 36.1\,dB to $\approx$34.2\,dB.

\subsubsection{ISAC --- Integrated Sensing and Communication}
\label{art2:sec:isac}

ISAC reuses the OFDM waveform for environment sensing~\cite{liu2022isac, zhang2022isac_overview}: range-Doppler analysis ($\Delta r{=}1.5$\,cm) with CFAR detection ($P_{\text{fa}}{=}10^{-4}$) computes a KL-divergence anomaly score; $s_i > 0.5$ triggers the MAPE-K Monitor, augmenting heartbeat-based failure detection at 15\% energy overhead.

\subsection{Theoretical Guarantee: CDL Pareto Bound}
\label{art2:sec:theorem}

We extend Neely's Lyapunov drift-plus-penalty framework~\cite{neely2010lyapunov} to bound the \emph{total lifecycle} carbon---not just operational carbon---by incorporating end-of-life emissions into the virtual queue.

\begin{theorem}[Carbon-Delivery-Lifecycle (CDL) Pareto Bound]
\label{art2:thm:cdl}
Define the total lifecycle carbon $C_{\text{tot}}(t) = C_{\text{op}}(t) + K(t) \cdot c_{\text{eol}}^{\text{eff}}$, where $K(t)$ is the number of node deaths at round~$t$ and $c_{\text{eol}}^{\text{eff}} = c_{\text{eol}} (1 - r_{\text{recycle}})$.
Update the virtual queue:
\begin{equation}
    Z(t+1) = \max\big\{0,\; Z(t) + C_{\text{tot}}(t) - \bar{c}\big\}
    \label{art2:eq:queue_update_cdl}
\end{equation}
For $V > 0$, the CALASH routing achieves simultaneously:
\begin{align}
    \text{(i)}  & \quad \bar{D} \geq \bar{D}^* - B/V  & \text{(near-optimal delivery)} \label{art2:eq:delivery_bound} \\
    \text{(ii)} & \quad \bar{C}_{\text{tot}} \leq \bar{c} + B/V  & \text{(lifecycle carbon budget)} \label{art2:eq:carbon_bound}
\end{align}
where $\bar{D}^*$ is the optimal delivery rate of any static policy, $\bar{c} = C_{\text{budget}}/T$, and $B = \frac{1}{2}\max_t\{(C_{\text{tot}}(t) - \bar{c})^2\}$.
Moreover, the Pareto front $(\bar{D}(V),\, \bar{C}_{\text{tot}}(V))$ is convex and continuously traceable by varying $V \in (0, \infty)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $L(t) = Z(t)^2/2$.
The one-step drift satisfies $\Delta(t) \leq B + Z(t)(C_{\text{tot}}(t) - \bar{c})$.
Adding penalty $V \cdot (-D(t))$ and noting that CALASH greedily minimizes the RHS via Eq.~\eqref{art2:eq:lyapunov_decision} with $\ell_j$, the drift-plus-penalty bound becomes $B - V \cdot \bar{D}^*$.
Telescoping over $T$ rounds yields Eq.~\eqref{art2:eq:delivery_bound}; queue stability gives Eq.~\eqref{art2:eq:carbon_bound}.
Eliminating $V$ via $B/V = \bar{D}^* - \bar{D}$ shows $\bar{C} = \bar{c} + (\bar{D}^* - \bar{D})$, a linear (hence convex) trade-off; higher-order terms preserve convexity~\cite{neely2010lyapunov}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{art2:sec:setup}

\textbf{Simulation parameters.}
200 nodes, $200 \times 200$\,m, BS at $(100, 250)$, $E_0 = 0.5$\,J, 5{,}000 rounds, disaster at round 2{,}000 ($r_d = 100$\,m, center of area).
Each experiment is repeated with 30 independent random seeds (seeds 42--71); we report mean $\pm$ 95\% Confidence Interval (CI) via the $t$-distribution.

\textbf{Protocols compared.}
We evaluate 13 protocol variants:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{7 baselines} spanning four generations of WSN routing: LEACH~\cite{heinzelman2000leach}, LEACH-1hop, EE-LEACH~\cite{bakaraniya2013eeleach}, ABC-ACO~\cite{elkhediri2024abcaco}, EERP~\cite{biswas2018eerp}, Q-Routing~\cite{boyan1994qrouting, watkins1992qlearning}, and \textbf{RIS-DRL}~\cite{huang2020ris_drl, alhilo2024ris_uav, huang2021ris}.
    RIS-DRL shares CALASH's 6G physical layer (THz + RIS) but uses a standard DQN without carbon awareness, lifecycle integration, or self-healing, isolating CALASH's algorithmic contributions.
    \item \textbf{CALASH}: full framework (all four pillars + 6G technologies).
    \item \textbf{5 ablation variants}: CALASH-NoCO2, CALASH-NoSH, CALASH-NoCADR, CALASH-NoLCI, CALASH-NoTHz.
\end{itemize}
All protocols share the same energy model, topology, and disaster event~\cite{dietrich2009lifetime}.
Baselines do not use compressive sensing (fidelity = 1.0) as their original formulations do not include it.

\textbf{Metrics.}
Half-death round (50\% nodes dead), PDR (delivered/generated), operational carbon (gCO$_2$eq), data fidelity ($1{-}\text{NMSE}$), LCI (gCO$_2$eq/pkt, Eq.~\eqref{art2:eq:lci_formal}), energy efficiency (pkt/J), and Jain's fairness index~\cite{jain1984fairness}.

\textbf{Statistical significance.}
We use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945} (non-parametric, paired, $\alpha = 0.05$) to compare CALASH against each baseline, following simulation best practices~\cite{law2015simulation, jain1991art}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{art2:sec:conclusion}

We presented CALASH, the first WSN routing framework jointly minimizing lifecycle carbon intensity through four pillars: CADR, CARE, SHDR, and LSE.
Evaluation (30~seeds, 13~variants, 5{,}000~rounds) shows \textbf{60\% longer lifetime} vs.\ LEACH, \textbf{32\% lower LCI} vs.\ the best traditional baseline, and \textbf{82.9\% PDR} with perfect fidelity.
The ablation confirms CADR as the dominant pillar ($+59\%$ LCI when removed), and the RIS-DRL comparison reveals a controlled energy--carbon--resilience trade-off.
The key insight is that \emph{sending fewer bits} via compressive sensing is the most effective lever for reducing WSN carbon footprint.

Future work:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Replace the DQN with a lightweight linear policy and use ISAC passive failure detection to close the RIS-DRL gap.
    \item Validate on a 20-node ESP32-S3 + LoRa testbed with INA219 power monitoring~\cite{uk_carbon_api}.
    \item Extend to cascading multi-disaster scenarios and energy-harvesting nodes~\cite{huang2013energy}.
    \item Cross-validate THz channel estimates via ns-3 co-simulation~\cite{ns3_thz}.
\end{enumerate}
