# Bell Mobility 6G Strategy, Evidence Gaps, and Thesis-Fit Assessment

## Executive synthesis

Bell Mobility does not have a publicly disclosed, operator-branded 6G roadmap as of 2024–2026. The best-supported interpretation is that Bell is building a 6G foundation through 5G Standalone, 5G+ Advanced, AI-native RAN field testing, Cloud/Open RAN partnerships, sovereign AI infrastructure, non-terrestrial network work, and standards-body participation, while avoiding a public 6G deployment date, 6G R&D budget, or Bell-authored 6G technical white paper ([BCE 2024 Annual Financial Report](https://prd-www.bce.ca/investors/AR-2024/2024-bce-annual-financial-report.pdf), [Bell 5G+ Advanced launch](https://www.bce.ca/news-and-media/newsroom?article=bell-introduces-5g-advanced-its-fastest-and-most-advanced-network-yet), [Ericsson-Bell AI-native link adaptation](https://www.ericsson.com/en/press-releases/6/2025/ericsson-and-bell-canada-successfully-test-ai-native-link-adaptation-to-boost-network-speed-and-efficiency), [Nokia-Bell Cloud/Open RAN partnership](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/bell-canada-and-nokia-expand-5g-partnership-to-accelerate-innovation-with-cloud-and-open-ran-deployment-mwc25/)).

The strongest confirmed Bell 6G-adjacent priorities are AI-native radio optimization, cloud-native and eventually open RAN, spectrum-efficient 5G-Advanced evolution, MEC/edge compute, NTN/direct-to-cell coverage, and network energy efficiency. Bell and Ericsson’s April 2025 live-network AI-native link adaptation test reported up to 20% higher downlink throughput and up to 10% better spectral efficiency, making it Bell’s most concrete public AI-RAN/6G-adjacent technical proof point ([Ericsson-Bell AI-native link adaptation](https://www.ericsson.com/en/press-releases/6/2025/ericsson-and-bell-canada-successfully-test-ai-native-link-adaptation-to-boost-network-speed-and-efficiency)).

The weakest confirmed public Bell 6G priorities are sub-THz access, RIS, ISAC, explicit carbon-aware routing, and first-party 6G patent origination. No Bell Canada-assigned 2023–2026 patent filings were confirmed for RIS, THz/sub-THz, AI-native RAN, O-RAN/RIC, zero-trust, ISAC, or carbon-aware routing; Bell’s apparent IP model is operator integration and vendor-partner deployment rather than first-party patent creation ([GreyB Bell Canada patent analysis](https://insights.greyb.com/bell-canada-patents/), [Google Patents Bell legacy routing patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8300798B1/en), [Google Patents Bell wireless offload patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10237757B2/en)).

For a Polytechnique Montréal PhD researcher presenting 15 minutes to Bell engineers and executives in 2026, the most resonant framing is not “generic 6G.” The winning frame is “energy-secure autonomous networks for Canadian geography,” anchored in Bell’s Scope 1+2 reduction target, AI-native RAN field work, Cloud/Open RAN roadmap, NG9-1-1/public safety obligations, and Canada’s rural/Indigenous connectivity constraints ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf), [CRTC Telecom Decision 2025-291](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2025/2025-291.htm), [CRTC Telecom Regulatory Policy 2024-328](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2024/2024-328.htm)).

## Bell’s publicly stated 6G-adjacent priorities

### Standards and roadmap commitments

Bell is a founding member of the ATIS Next G Alliance, the North American 6G pre-standardization body launched in October 2020 ([ATIS Next G Alliance launch](https://atis.org/press-releases/atis-launches-next-g-alliance-to-advance-north-american-leadership-in-6g/)). The Next G Alliance has published 2025–2026 work relevant to Bell’s environment, including North American spectrum groundwork, ISAC readiness, sustainable 6G, vertical markets, and 7–24 GHz channel measurement activity, but Bell’s individual submissions or delegate positions in those working groups are not public ([Next G Alliance spectrum groundwork](https://nextgalliance.org/laying-the-groundwork-for-6g-in-north-america/), [ATIS Next G Alliance ISAC report](https://atis.org/press-releases/next-g-alliance-releases-landmark-report-on-integrated-sensing-and-communication-for-6g-innovation-enabling-a-paradigm-shift-in-next-generation-wireless-networks/)).

Bell has publicly confirmed participation in 3GPP and NGMN historically, and a 2024 Bell public-safety article states that Bell plans to prepare for the eventual 3GPP standard of 6G connectivity ([Bell standards participation press release](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/bell-applauds-government-commitment-to-next-generation-5g-wireless-677434333.html), [Bell MCPTT article](https://explore.business.bell.ca/learning-resources/mission-critical-push-to-talk-key-element-public-safety-communications)). The gap is material: public sources do not disclose Bell’s 3GPP Release 20/21 contribution count, working-group roles, or technical positions on 6G study items.

Bell’s most explicit roadmap commitment is the 2026 launch of 5G+ Advanced, which Bell describes as its fastest network and a foundation “paving the way towards 6G” ([Bell 5G+ Advanced launch](https://www.bce.ca/news-and-media/newsroom?article=bell-introduces-5g-advanced-its-fastest-and-most-advanced-network-yet)). Technically, this matters because 5G Standalone, mid-band 3800 MHz, slicing, low-latency control, and AI-native network operations are prerequisites for many 6G architecture concepts rather than merely marketing language.

### White papers and press releases

No Bell-authored 6G technical white paper was confirmed. Bell’s public record instead consists of partner announcements and network-evolution releases, especially Ericsson’s AI-native link adaptation test, Nokia’s Cloud/Open RAN deal, Cohere Technologies’ OTFS/USM work with Bell Ventures, and AST SpaceMobile direct-to-cell activity ([Ericsson-Bell AI-native link adaptation](https://www.ericsson.com/en/press-releases/6/2025/ericsson-and-bell-canada-successfully-test-ai-native-link-adaptation-to-boost-network-speed-and-efficiency), [Nokia-Bell Cloud/Open RAN partnership](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/bell-canada-and-nokia-expand-5g-partnership-to-accelerate-innovation-with-cloud-and-open-ran-deployment-mwc25/), [Cohere Bell Ventures investment](https://www.cohere-tech.com/cohere-technologies-receives-funding-from-bell-ventures-for-5g-and-6g/), [Bell direct-to-cell milestone](https://explore.business.bell.ca/mobility-spotlight/direct-to-cell-major-milestone-canadian-connectivity)).

Bell Ventures’ 2023 investment in Cohere Technologies is important because Cohere explicitly stated that Bell’s interest was intended to accelerate OTFS as a candidate waveform for 6G and for satellite connectivity ([Cohere Bell Ventures investment](https://www.cohere-tech.com/cohere-technologies-receives-funding-from-bell-ventures-for-5g-and-6g/)). Cohere later reported successful SU/MU-MIMO coordinated scheduling tests over open interfaces on Bell’s 850 MHz 5G SA network near Bell’s Mississauga labs, which is a concrete open-interface and spectral-efficiency experiment even though it is not a public Bell-authored 6G roadmap ([Cohere Bell trial results](https://www.cohere-tech.com/bell2025/)).

### Bell Labs Canada and research agenda

There is no evidence that BCE currently operates a public research organization called “Bell Labs Canada” in the historic Bell Labs sense. Nokia Bell Labs is a separate Nokia entity, and Bell Canada’s research footprint appears through Bell Ventures, vendor labs, standards participation, internal network engineering, AI partnerships, and sovereign compute infrastructure rather than a named Bell Labs Canada 6G program ([Nokia Bell Labs 6G research](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/research/6g-networks/)).

Bell’s academic partnerships from 2024–2026 are more AI/compute/security oriented than wireless PHY oriented. Bell announced a three-year AI partnership with Mila, AI supercomputing collaboration with Simon Fraser University, an AI supercomputing MOU with Queen’s University, and a cybersecurity investment with the University of New Brunswick’s McKenna Institute, but no public Bell partnership specifically on THz propagation, RIS, ISAC, or 6G PHY research with Polytechnique Montréal, McGill, Concordia, Waterloo, or University of Toronto was confirmed ([Bell-Mila partnership](https://explore.business.bell.ca/news-and-events/bell-mila-join-forces-drive-ai-innovation), [Bell-SFU AI supercomputing MOU](https://explore.business.bell.ca/news-and-events/advancing-canada-ai-supercomputing-ecosystem-simon-fraser-university), [Bell-Queen’s AI supercomputing MOU](https://www.bce.ca/news-and-media/newsroom?article=bell-and-queens-university-joining-forces-to-build-sovereign-ai-supercomputing-infrastructure-in-can), [Bell-UNB cybersecurity investment](https://explore.business.bell.ca/news-and-events/investing-1-million-strengthen-canada-cybersecurity-talent-pipeline)).

## Canadian spectrum and regulatory constraints

Canada has not launched a standalone 6G consultation as of mid-2026. ISED’s 6G preparation is occurring through the Spectrum Outlook 2023–2027, mmWave consultation, Canadian Table of Frequency Allocations updates, and spectrum-above-95-GHz decisions rather than a dedicated Bell/ISED 6G docket ([ISED Spectrum Outlook 2023–2027](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/spectrum-allocation/spectrum-outlook-2023-2027), [ISED 26/38 GHz consultation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/spectrum-allocation/spectrum-auctions/spectrum-auction-publications/consultation-26-ghz-and-38-ghz-bands)).

The upper mid-band 7.125–8.4 GHz range is a major WRC-27 IMT study candidate and likely more important for Canadian 6G coverage economics than sub-THz access, because it offers a better capacity-coverage compromise for large inter-site distances ([ISED proposed CTFA revisions](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/consultations/proposed-revisions-canadian-table-frequency-allocations-2026-edition), [GSMA 6G spectrum demand analysis](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/6g-mobile-networks-will-need-up-to-three-times-todays-spectrum-to-meet-surging-data-demands-new-gsma-report-shows/)). Canada has not yet formally consulted on commercial 7–15 GHz mobile service, which creates uncertainty for Bell’s eventual 6G macro-layer planning.

Canada’s mmWave framework remains an active bottleneck. ISED’s 2025 consultation covers 26 GHz and 38 GHz, considers spectrum caps of 800 MHz or 1200 MHz, and explicitly asks whether current 5G mmWave spectrum will serve 6G or whether new bands are required ([ISED 26/38 GHz consultation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/spectrum-allocation/spectrum-auctions/spectrum-auction-publications/consultation-26-ghz-and-38-ghz-bands)). Bell’s public spectrum-cost position is that Canadian fees are high and future auctions should avoid artificially limiting supply, but no Bell 6G-specific spectrum submission was confirmed ([Bell parliamentary brief](https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/451/FINA/Brief/BR13582294/br-external/BellCanada-e.pdf)).

Sub-THz policy is more research-enabling than deployment-ready. ISED approved licence-exempt use in bands including 116–122.25 GHz, 174.8–182 GHz, 185–190 GHz, and 244–246 GHz, while W-band and D-band licensed backhaul frameworks remain pending ([ISED spectrum above 95 GHz decision](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/decision-technical-and-policy-framework-frequency-bands-above-95-ghz)). For Bell, sub-THz is realistic first as dense urban hotspot access, indoor enterprise/stadium connectivity, or point-to-point Xhaul, not rural wide-area coverage.

Canadian geography shifts Bell’s 6G priorities toward coverage resilience, NTN, energy efficiency, and rural economics. CRTC data show that 96.4% of Canadian households have 50/10 Mbps access overall, but only 69.6% in the three territories and 65.7% on First Nations reserves, making northern and Indigenous connectivity a policy constraint rather than an optional market segment ([CRTC Indigenous connectivity announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/radio-television-telecommunications/news/2026/03/crtc-making-it-easier-to-connect-indigenous-communities-to-high-speed-internet-and-cellphone-services.html)). CRTC’s 2024 Broadband Fund policy now requires applicants to obtain Indigenous community consent for funded builds, and 2025–2026 CRTC actions are creating dedicated Indigenous and Far North connectivity mechanisms ([CRTC Telecom Regulatory Policy 2024-328](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2024/2024-328.htm), [CRTC Telecom Regulatory Policy 2025-9](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2025/2025-9.htm)).

## Sustainability: what Bell has committed versus what is technically shown

BCE’s 2030 science-based targets include a 58% reduction in absolute Scope 1+2 emissions from 2020, 64% of supplier spend having science-based targets by 2026, and a 42% reduction in Scope 3 emissions excluding purchased goods and services by 2030 ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf)). BCE reported a 27% Scope 1+2 reduction by 2024, 34% of supplier spend with science-based targets, and 2025 carbon-neutral Scope 1+2 operations via carbon credits and renewable energy instruments ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf), [Bell for Better](https://www.bell.ca/bell-for-better)).

The key ESG gap for 6G is embodied carbon. Scope 3 represented 91% of BCE’s 2024 footprint, and purchased goods and services represented 59% of total footprint, yet purchased goods and services are excluded from BCE’s Scope 3 reduction target ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf)). This matters for 6G because densification, Open RAN servers, radios, antennas, edge compute, and new RIS/sub-THz hardware could shift Bell’s decarbonization challenge from operational electricity to equipment lifecycle carbon.

Bell’s network energy progress is credible but not yet tied to AI feature-level carbon accounting. BCE reported 2024 energy intensity of 74 MWh-equivalent per petabyte of network usage, down from 103 in 2023, while Ericsson’s Bell test reported spectral efficiency gains and Nokia’s MantaRay/energy product literature describes sleep-mode capabilities, but Bell has not disclosed per-feature MWh savings, tCO2e reductions, or sleep-mode utilization rates across its network ([BCE 2024 Annual Financial Report](https://prd-www.bce.ca/investors/AR-2024/2024-bce-annual-financial-report.pdf), [Ericsson-Bell AI-native link adaptation](https://www.ericsson.com/en/press-releases/6/2025/ericsson-and-bell-canada-successfully-test-ai-native-link-adaptation-to-boost-network-speed-and-efficiency), [Nokia AI sleep-mode discussion](https://www.nokia.com/blog/ai-builds-the-foundation-for-zero-energy-use-at-zero-traffic/)).

Carbon-aware routing is a visible research gap. Bell’s public sustainability disclosures discuss virtualization, remote monitoring, ISO 50001 energy management, AI partnerships, and facility optimization, but no Bell disclosure was found for carbon-aware network routing, grid-carbon-aware load balancing, or real-time RAN decisions based on marginal electricity emissions ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf), [BCE 2024 Annual Financial Report](https://prd-www.bce.ca/investors/AR-2024/2024-bce-annual-financial-report.pdf)).

## O-RAN, RIC, security, and latency

Bell’s O-RAN posture is best described as “Cloud RAN now, Open RAN path next.” Bell’s Nokia partnership includes Cloud RAN software on Red Hat OpenShift, Dell PowerEdge servers, AirScale radios with Open Fronthaul capability, and Nokia MantaRay NM, giving Bell a credible evolutionary path to Open RAN without public evidence of a commercial near-RT RIC deployment yet ([Nokia-Bell Cloud/Open RAN partnership](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/bell-canada-and-nokia-expand-5g-partnership-to-accelerate-innovation-with-cloud-and-open-ran-deployment-mwc25/)).

TELUS is ahead of Bell in public Canadian RIC/O-RAN deployment evidence. TELUS and Samsung announced Canada’s first commercial 5G vRAN/Open RAN deployment in 2024 and Canada’s first commercial AI-powered RIC in 2025, including KPI anomaly detection, energy saving, and load balancing use cases ([Samsung-TELUS vRAN/Open RAN announcement](https://www.samsung.com/global/business/networks/insights/press-release/0214-telus-partners-with-samsung-to-build-canadas-first-5g-virtualized-ran-open-ran-network/), [Samsung-TELUS AI RIC announcement](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-and-telus-collaborate-on-canadas-first-ai-powered-ran-intelligent-controller-to-optimize-network-performance-and-energy-efficiency)).

The O-RAN near-RT RIC operates in the 10 ms to 1 second control window, while non-RT RIC/rApps run above 1 second and DU-level real-time control runs below 10 ms ([RimeDo Labs near-RT RIC explainer](https://rimedolabs.com/blog/o-ran-near-real-time-ric/)). Bell’s Ericsson AI-native link adaptation appears below the RIC layer in real-time baseband control, while Nokia MantaRay is a non-RT management layer; the missing public layer is Bell’s near-RT RIC/xApp deployment ([Ericsson-Bell AI-native link adaptation](https://www.ericsson.com/en/press-releases/6/2025/ericsson-and-bell-canada-successfully-test-ai-native-link-adaptation-to-boost-network-speed-and-efficiency), [Nokia-Bell Cloud/Open RAN partnership](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/bell-canada-and-nokia-expand-5g-partnership-to-accelerate-innovation-with-cloud-and-open-ran-deployment-mwc25/)).

O-RAN security is a major collaboration opportunity because the architecture expands the attack surface through open interfaces, multi-vendor components, O-Cloud infrastructure, RIC applications, and AI/ML model pipelines. O-RAN Alliance WG11 reported more than 160 distinct threats and added AI/ML threat categories such as poisoning, model inversion, model stealing, membership inference, and AI supply-chain attacks ([O-RAN Alliance security update 2025](https://www.o-ran.org/blog/o-ran-alliance-security-update-2025), [O-RAN Alliance security update 2026](https://www.o-ran.org/blog/o-ran-alliance-security-update-2026)).

Canadian regulation increases the value of zero-trust and formally verifiable autonomous-network controls. Bill C-26 and its successor Bill C-8 would give government powers over telecom security, supply-chain controls, vulnerability mitigation, incident reporting, and cybersecurity records, while CSE’s Telecoms Cyber Resilience Program focuses on standards, architecture, and supply-chain risks in telecom networks ([Dentons Bill C-8 analysis](https://www.dentonsdata.com/from-bill-c-26-to-bill-c-8-house-of-commons-reintroduces-key-cybersecurity-legislation/), [Public Safety Canada telecom cyber resilience briefing](https://www.publicsafety.gc.ca/cnt/trnsprnc/brfng-mtrls/prlmntry-bndrs/20250226-1/12-en.aspx), [CSE annual report 2024–2025](https://www.cse-cst.gc.ca/en/accountability/transparency/reports/communications-security-establishment-canada-annual-report-2024-2025)).

## RIS, sub-THz, and ISAC deployment realism in Canada

Sub-THz 100–300 GHz is not a rural macro coverage solution for Bell. Ericsson’s 6G spectrum discussion positions sub-THz as useful for extreme data rates and short-range high-capacity links, while lower bands remain essential for coverage, capacity, and mobility ([Ericsson sub-THz 6G discussion](https://www.ericsson.com/en/6g/spectrum/sub-thz), [Ericsson 6G spectrum white paper](https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/white-papers/6g-spectrum-enabling-the-future-mobile-life-beyond-2030)).

RIS is realistic for Bell only if the research problem is framed around power, robustness, and specific coverage economics. Passive RIS can have very low power draw in prototype literature, while active RIS can overcome severe path loss at the cost of additional power and noise, creating a direct ESG trade-off for Bell ([IEEE VTC 2024 RIS power analysis](http://ieeevtc.org/vtc2024spring/DATA/PID2024002335.pdf), [arXiv active versus passive RIS comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07510), [University of Bremen active RIS ISAC energy paper](https://www.ant.uni-bremen.de/sixcms/media.php/102/15020/2024_JWCN_Rihan.pdf)). The Canadian-specific research gap is winter: ice, snow, radome effects, freeze-thaw cycling, and rural mounting constraints are under-characterized in public literature.

ISAC is strategically relevant to Bell if tied to public safety, NG9-1-1, PSBN, and disaster response rather than abstract sensing. ATIS’s 2025 ISAC readiness work frames ISAC as a 6G capability, while Bell publicly markets public safety, NG9-1-1, PTT, and first-responder broadband offerings ([ATIS Next G Alliance ISAC report](https://atis.org/press-releases/next-g-alliance-releases-landmark-report-on-integrated-sensing-and-communication-for-6g-innovation-enabling-a-paradigm-shift-in-next-generation-wireless-networks/), [Bell public safety portfolio](https://business.bell.ca/shop/medium-large/mobility/public-safety), [Bell PSBN article](https://explore.business.bell.ca/learning-resources/why-canada-needs-public-safety-broadband-network-psbn)). The strongest Bell-relevant ISAC use cases are flood sensing, survivor detection, UAV detection, smart highways, wildfire monitoring, and first-responder situational awareness.

## Thesis contribution fit

The contribution descriptions below are treated as user-provided thesis claims, not independently verified source facts. Bell would require thesis chapters, code, datasets, proof artifacts, reproducibility evidence, and freedom-to-operate review before collaboration or licensing.

### GAPF

User-provided claim: GAPF is a 1,473-parameter MARL meta-controller using GNN topology encoding for energy-efficient WSN routing, with 98% PDR, 2–3.5x energy reduction, 13x memory reduction versus baselines, and TinyML compatibility on ARM Cortex-M4.

Bell fit: GAPF is relevant if framed less as “WSN routing” and more as “sub-1W distributed inference for IoT, rural sensors, and RAN-adjacent edge control.” Bell’s public IoT/Smart Core direction and 5G support for high device density create a plausible business context for TinyML routing and local autonomy ([Bell IoT modernization article](https://explore.business.bell.ca/learning-resources/modernizing-iot-how-evolving-mobile-networks-unlock-new-business-potential)). The strongest Bell teams would be Bell Mobility network engineering, Bell IoT/Smart Core, Bell Public Safety if tied to resilient sensor meshes, and Bell AI Fabric edge inference teams.

Readiness gap: GAPF needs a telecom-grade validation bridge. Bell would need results on NB-IoT/LTE-M/5G mMTC traffic models, comparison with standard low-power routing baselines, proof that the 1,473-parameter controller remains stable under packet loss and mobility, energy measurements on real Cortex-M4-class hardware, and a security story for adversarial or compromised nodes.

Collaboration hook: “A sub-1W, TinyML-compatible controller for resilient rural/Indigenous/community sensor networks that reduces truck rolls and network energy while preserving delivery reliability.” This hook maps GAPF to Bell’s rural coverage and IoT operations rather than to Bell’s currently unproven RIS/sub-THz roadmap.

### CALASH

User-provided claim: CALASH is carbon-aware lifecycle routing integrating ISO 14040 embodied, operational, and end-of-life carbon, using Lyapunov-DQN with a proven Pareto CDL bound and adaptive compressive sensing modulated by real-time grid carbon intensity, with 60% longer lifetime and 33% LCI reduction.

Bell fit: CALASH is the strongest immediate Bell fit because it directly targets the gap between BCE’s carbon ambition and the lack of disclosed carbon-aware routing or lifecycle carbon optimization. BCE reports Scope 1+2 and Scope 3 targets, but purchased goods and services dominate total footprint and lack a binding reduction target, making lifecycle-aware optimization a highly relevant differentiator ([BCE 2024 Climate Action Report](https://pfo-www.bce.ca/responsibility/overview/2024-bce-climate-action-report.pdf)).

Readiness gap: CALASH must be translated from research routing into Bell-operable decision domains. Bell would need a testbed showing carbon-aware decisions do not degrade SLA, emergency traffic, latency, or reliability; ISO 14040 assumptions must be auditable; grid-carbon inputs must map to Canadian provincial electricity intensity; and the method must separate operational carbon reductions from embodied-carbon deferral due to longer equipment lifetime.

Collaboration hook: “Carbon-aware lifecycle control for low-power IoT and RAN-adjacent edge infrastructure that reduces total lifecycle carbon, not only electricity.” This is highly differentiated because Bell’s ESG reporting does not yet provide a technical pathway for embodied carbon or carbon-aware routing.

### CASTER-ZT

User-provided claim: CASTER-ZT is a zero-trust shielded GNN decision framework for autonomous recovery under adversarial conditions, with formal bounded admissibility, 10 proved properties, 74–98% rogue detection, zero false positives, and 3.07 ms per-decision latency within an O-RAN 10 ms budget.

Bell fit: CASTER-ZT is the most differentiated thesis contribution for Bell’s security, O-RAN, and public-safety teams. O-RAN WG11 has identified AI/ML and interface threats, near-RT RIC has a 10 ms to 1 second operating window, and Canadian cyber rules are moving toward stronger supply-chain and incident-reporting obligations ([O-RAN Alliance security update 2025](https://www.o-ran.org/blog/o-ran-alliance-security-update-2025), [RimeDo Labs near-RT RIC explainer](https://rimedolabs.com/blog/o-ran-near-real-time-ric/), [Dentons Bill C-8 analysis](https://www.dentonsdata.com/from-bill-c-26-to-bill-c-8-house-of-commons-reintroduces-key-cybersecurity-legislation/)).

Readiness gap: CASTER-ZT must prove compatibility with realistic O-RAN interfaces and threat models. Bell would need a mapped threat model for rogue xApps, compromised E2 nodes, malicious telemetry, adversarial GNN inputs, vendor supply-chain attacks, and recovery actions; it would also need a hardware/software-in-the-loop demo showing that the 3.07 ms decision time remains valid with authentication, policy checks, logging, and container/network overhead.

Collaboration hook: “A formally bounded zero-trust guardrail for autonomous RAN recovery that can stay inside near-RT RIC timing while detecting rogue behavior without false positives.” This should be pitched as a 3–5 year research collaboration, not a near-term product.

## Bottom line

Bell’s real public 6G strategy is not a named 6G program. It is an integration strategy: deploy 5G+ Advanced, prove AI-native RAN with Ericsson, move toward Cloud/Open RAN with Nokia, build sovereign AI/edge compute, use NTN for Canadian coverage, and manage ESG/regulatory pressure while standards mature.

For thesis positioning, CALASH should lead because it maps most directly to Bell’s measurable ESG and lifecycle-carbon gaps. CASTER-ZT should be second because it is differentiated and timely for O-RAN/autonomous-network security under Canadian critical-infrastructure regulation. GAPF should be third but still important if framed as TinyML-grade edge autonomy for rural IoT, public safety sensors, and low-power mMTC rather than as abstract WSN routing.
