# Literature Review

## 1. Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are foundational in monitoring and communication systems, especially in dynamic scenarios such as disaster monitoring due to their distributed sensing capabilities. Extensive research has been conducted on energy efficiency, routing strategies, sustainability, and integration with future wireless technologies. However, there remains a gap at the intersection of autonomous routing, lifecycle sustainability, disaster resilience, and integration with 6G-envisioned network paradigms.

## 2. Energy-Efficient and AI-Driven Routing

Energy constraints are a fundamental design concern in WSNs, given the limited battery capacity of sensor nodes and the impracticality of frequent recharging or replacement. Routing protocols have evolved from static and hierarchical routing to intelligent, adaptive AI-enhanced methods capable of improving energy usage and prolonging network lifetime. For instance, modular AI-based routing frameworks have been proposed that combine reinforcement learning (RL), supervised learning, and meta-heuristic techniques such as Genetic Algorithms (GA) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to adapt routing decisions dynamically in real-time network conditions. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The literature highlights significant improvements in energy efficiency and network performance through AI-driven routing, but these studies typically do not incorporate sustainability at the lifecycle level or evaluate performance under conditions like disaster scenarios.

## 3. Self-Healing and Fault-Tolerant Routing

Self-healing routing mechanisms aim to preserve network connectivity and performance despite node failures or communication disruptions by dynamically restructuring routing paths or clusters. Clustered self-healing protocols enhance reliability and resilience, but they often do not consider embedded sustainability indicators or disaster recovery challenges.

## 4. 6G Integration and Future Network Paradigms

The emergence of 6G promises ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC), pervasive artificial intelligence, and network slicing capabilities. Research suggests that AI integration into 6G communication systems enables more secure and adaptive network operations. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} Additionally, studies on sustainable 6G architectures emphasize energy awareness and carbon footprint reduction strategies. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3} However, most work focuses on macro or infrastructure-level network paradigms rather than sensor-level adaptations suitable for constrained WSN environments.

## 5. Sustainability and Carbon-Awareness

While energy efficiency is a frequent focus in WSN research, comprehensive sustainability considerations — such as embodied carbon, lifecycle assessment, and circular economy principles — are largely absent at the protocol level. Research in broader communication systems underscores the importance of sustainable design practices but has not been extensively applied to WSN routing protocols or integrated networking strategies.

## 6. Disaster Monitoring Applications

WSNs play a significant role in disaster monitoring applications, including flood detection, landslide prediction, and environmental sensing. However, literature reveals that many of these systems prioritize accurate sensing and basic network connectivity over robust, adaptive networking solutions that can withstand failures and operational disruptions typical in disasters.

## 7. Gap Synthesis

Analysis of the literature confirms a broad coverage of energy-aware routing, AI-enhanced adaptability, sustainable network design, and high-level communication paradigms. However, there is no comprehensive framework that unifies autonomous routing optimization, self-healing resilience, lifecycle sustainability, and disaster resilience within a single integrated solution. This lack of holistic integration justifies the focused research direction of this thesis.
